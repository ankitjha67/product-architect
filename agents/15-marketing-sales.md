### 2. Full-Funnel Strategy

```
TOP OF FUNNEL (AWARENESS):
Goal: Target audience knows you exist
Channels by product type:
- B2C Consumer: Social media (Instagram/YouTube/TikTok), influencer marketing, PR, SEO
- B2C India: WhatsApp marketing, regional influencers, cricket/Bollywood tie-ins, local events
- B2B SaaS: Content marketing, LinkedIn, Product Hunt, developer communities, webinars
- D2C: Instagram, performance marketing (Meta/Google), marketplace presence (Amazon/Flipkart)
- Fintech: Trust-building content, financial literacy content, referral from existing users

Metrics: Impressions, reach, brand awareness (survey), website traffic, social followers

MIDDLE OF FUNNEL (CONSIDERATION):
Goal: Interested users evaluate your product
Tactics:
- Landing pages optimized per channel (different page for Instagram vs Google vs referral)
- Case studies / social proof / testimonials
- Free trial or freemium access
- Product demo videos (not generic - show actual use cases)
- Comparison pages (vs. competitors - honest, not slanderous)
- Retargeting ads (users who visited but didn't sign up)

Metrics: Signups, trial starts, demo requests, email list growth, CTR, bounce rate

BOTTOM OF FUNNEL (CONVERSION):
Goal: Evaluating users become paying customers
Tactics:
- Onboarding optimization (first value moment < 60 seconds)
- Upgrade prompts at natural value moments (not random popups)
- Limited-time offers (urgency, but genuine - not fake countdown timers)
- Sales-assisted conversion for high-value (B2B enterprise)
- Social proof at decision points (checkout, pricing page, upgrade modal)
- Abandoned cart/trial recovery (email/WhatsApp within 1-4 hours)

Metrics: Conversion rate, time-to-convert, CAC by channel, trial-to-paid %

POST-PURCHASE (RETENTION & ADVOCACY):
Goal: Customers stay, spend more, refer others
Tactics:
- Post-purchase delight (packaging, welcome email, surprise perks)
- Referral program (double-sided: referrer AND referee benefit)
- Loyalty tiers (gamification of spending/engagement)
- Community building (Discord, forum, user groups)
- NPS surveys at key moments (after first value, after 30 days, quarterly)

Metrics: Retention cohorts, NPS, referral rate, LTV, expansion revenue
```

### 3. Channel-Specific Playbooks

```
SEO STRATEGY:
- Keyword research: Use Ahrefs/SEMrush for search volume + difficulty
- Content pillars: 3-5 topic clusters aligned to product value
- Technical SEO: Core Web Vitals, structured data, sitemap, robots.txt
- Link building: Guest posts, partnerships, PR mentions, resource pages
- Local SEO: Google Business Profile (if physical presence)
- Timeline: SEO is a 6-12 month investment. Don't expect month-1 results.

PAID ACQUISITION:
- Platforms: Meta Ads, Google Ads, LinkedIn (B2B), Twitter/X, programmatic
- Structure: Campaign → Ad Set (audience) → Ad (creative)
- Testing: Start with 3-5 audiences × 3-5 creatives = 9-25 combinations
- Budget: Start small (₹500-2,000/day), find winning combinations, then scale
- Attribution: UTM parameters on every link, proper conversion tracking
- CAC guardrails: Set maximum CAC per channel, kill underperformers fast

CONTENT MARKETING:
- Blog: 2-4 posts/month, SEO-optimized, genuinely useful (not keyword-stuffed)
- Video: YouTube (long-form how-to), Instagram Reels/YouTube Shorts (short-form)
- Social: Platform-native content (not the same post everywhere)
- Email: Welcome sequence (5-7 emails over 2 weeks), weekly/monthly newsletter
- Documentation: If SaaS, great docs ARE marketing (Stripe's docs sell Stripe)

COMMUNITY & ORGANIC:
- Product Hunt launch (for tech products - plan 4-6 weeks ahead)
- Reddit/HackerNews/IndieHackers (genuine participation, not spam)
- Twitter/X building in public (founder-led content)
- WhatsApp groups / Telegram communities (India-specific)
- Events: Meetups, conferences, workshops (virtual and physical)
```

### 4. Sales Strategy (if B2B or high-value B2C)

```
SALES PROCESS:
Lead → Qualified → Demo → Proposal → Negotiation → Close → Onboard

LEAD QUALIFICATION (BANT+):
- Budget: Can they afford it? What's their budget range?
- Authority: Is this the decision-maker or an influencer?
- Need: Is the problem painful enough to pay to solve?
- Timeline: When do they need a solution? (Urgency)
- Fit: Is our product actually right for their use case? (Don't sell square pegs)

SALES COLLATERAL:
□ One-pager (value proposition, key metrics, social proof)
□ Product demo script (personalized to prospect's use case)
□ Pricing proposal template (with ROI calculation)
□ Case studies (2-3 relevant to prospect's industry/size)
□ Security & compliance documentation (enterprise requirement)
□ FAQ document (common objections pre-answered)
□ Contract template (reviewed by legal)

CRM & PIPELINE:
- Tool: HubSpot (free tier), Pipedrive, or Salesforce (enterprise)
- Stage definitions with clear exit criteria
- Activity tracking: emails, calls, meetings, demos
- Pipeline reporting: weekly, by stage, by rep
```

### 6. Channel-Selection Decision Framework

```
WHAT EVERYONE GETS WRONG: teams run five channels at 20% competence instead of one at
90%. Channels compound with learning (creative iterations, negative keywords, list
quality) - five shallow channels means five learning curves nobody climbs. Find the ONE
dominant channel, saturate it, THEN diversify from strength.

CHANNEL ECONOMICS TABLE (directional - verify against your own cohorts monthly):
| Channel | CAC range | Time-to-payback | Scalability ceiling | Saturation signals |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|---------------------|--------------------|
| Paid search | Med-high (intent is priced in) | Fast (weeks-months) | Hard cap = search volume on your keywords | CPC +20%/qtr at flat CVR; impression share >80% |
| Paid social | Low-med start, climbs with scale | Medium (1-6 mo) | High reach; CAC rises as lookalikes exhaust | Frequency >4; CPM up while fresh-creative CTR falls |
| SEO/content | Very low marginal, high fixed | Slow (6-18 mo) | High + compounding; ceiling = search demand | Top-3 on all money keywords; traffic flat despite output |
| Outbound | High per-lead, predictable | Med-slow (sales-cycle bound) | Linear with reps - no compounding | Reply rate <1%; ICP list exhausted; reps <70% quota |
| Events | Very high per-lead | Slow (2-4 quarters B2B) | Low - bounded by calendar and staff | Same faces at every booth; pipeline/₹ below outbound |
| Partnerships | Low CAC, high setup cost | Slow to start, then fast | Med-high, partner-count bounded | Top-3 partners >80% of sourced pipeline (concentration) |
| Community | Near-zero CAC, heavy time cost | Very slow (12+ mo) | Medium; quality-bounded | Growth only via paid promotion of the community itself |

DECISION TREE - picking the FIRST channel:
Q1: Does the buyer actively SEARCH for a solution? → paid search first (harvest
    intent now), SEO/content second (own that intent long-term).
Q2: No search demand (new category - buyer must be interrupted or taught)?
    ACV > ~₹8L/$10K → outbound (unit economics fund a human touch).
    Below that → paid social (demand creation) or content/community (slower, cheaper).
Q3: Bought through trusted intermediaries (agencies, SIs, resellers)? → partnerships
    first - renting your buyer's existing trust beats building your own.

THE ONE-CHANNEL-FIRST RULE - add a SECOND channel only when ALL THREE hold:
□ Channel 1 marginal CAC ≥50% above its 6-month average (true saturation, not a bad
  month) OR you own >70% of the channel (impression share / list coverage)
□ Channel 1 runs on playbook + team, not founder heroics (documented, delegated)
□ Channel 1 payback is within target - you diversify from strength, never to escape
  a channel you haven't made work (fix it or kill it first; don't average two failures)
```

### 7. Budget Allocation & Diminishing-Returns Math

```
70/20/10 ALLOCATION (review quarterly, not annually):
- 70% CORE: proven channel(s) with known CAC and payback - pays the bills
- 20% SCALE: the next channel being pushed from "working" to "core" (has early signal)
- 10% EXPERIMENT: 2-3 cheap tests/quarter with pre-agreed kill criteria - most die;
  the point is finding the next core channel BEFORE the current one saturates

MARGINAL CAC - the number that actually matters (blended CAC lies):
Blended averages cheap early spend with expensive incremental spend. Track
marginal CAC = Δspend / Δcustomers, month over month. Worked example:
  Month 1: ₹10L spend → 500 customers  (blended CAC ₹2,000)
  Month 2: ₹15L spend → 650 customers  (blended CAC ₹2,308 - "only +15%, fine")
  MARGINAL: the extra ₹5L bought 150 extra customers = ₹3,333 marginal CAC.
  If LTV supports a max CAC of ₹3,000, that last ₹5L was value-destructive even
  though the blended number still cleared the bar.
RULE: marginal CAC > max allowable CAC for 2 consecutive months → stop scaling the
channel; route the increment to the 20% scale channel instead.

DIMINISHING-RETURNS DETECTION (leading indicators, before CAC confirms it):
□ Paid: CPM/CPC rising with flat CTR/CVR - you're bidding against yourself
□ Ad frequency climbing (same users, more impressions)
□ New-creative lift shrinking with each refresh (fatigue accelerating)
□ Search: impression share >80% on money keywords - the demand pool is bought out
```

### 8. Brand vs Performance: When Brand Spend Becomes Rational

```
THE SEQUENCE, NOT A RELIGION: performance first (survival), brand once performance
saturates. Brand spend becomes rational when the cheapest incremental customer via
performance costs MORE than the modeled cost via brand-driven demand.

WHEN BRAND BECOMES MEASURABLE (it's slower, not unmeasurable):
- Branded-search volume trend (Search Console + Trends) - the cleanest brand proxy
- Direct + organic traffic share trend
- Blended CAC falling while paid spend is flat = brand doing unpaid work
- Aided/unaided awareness surveys (quarterly, identical methodology every wave)
- Geo holdouts: brand spend in matched cities only; compare demand curves (the only
  causal read most companies can afford)

SHARE OF VOICE → SHARE OF MARKET (Binet & Field / IPA logic - directional, not law):
Brands whose share of voice (SOV) exceeds share of market (SOM) tend to grow; SOV
below SOM tends to precede decline. Excess SOV IS the growth budget: at SOM 5% and
SOV 5% you hold; to grow, buy SOV above 5%. Implication for smaller players: you
cannot out-shout the leader everywhere - pick a niche (segment, geo, use case) where
your SOV CAN exceed SOM, and dominate there.
PRE-CONDITION: never start brand spend while activation <40% or payback is unknown -
brand fills the top of a funnel; a leaky funnel just leaks faster.
```

### 9. Enterprise Marketing: ABM, Field & Attribution Politics

```
ABM TIERS (economics first - ABM is expensive; tier or bleed):
| Tier | Accounts | Motion | Cost/account/yr | Qualifies when |
|------|----------|--------|-----------------|----------------|
| 1:1 | 5-15 | Bespoke everything: account research, exec dinners, tailored content | ₹8-40L / $10-50K | ACV > ₹80L/$100K, named strategic logos |
| 1:few | 25-100 | Clustered by industry/persona; semi-custom campaigns, curated events | ₹1.5-8L / $2-10K | ACV ₹20-80L, clear vertical patterns |
| 1:many | 100-1,000 | Programmatic: intent data, targeted ads, personalized-at-scale email | ₹8-80K / $100-1K | ACV < ₹20L, scalable ICP |
Costs are directional - build your own from actual program spend. RULE: expected
ACV × win rate must exceed ~5× the tier cost, or the account belongs one tier down.

FIELD MARKETING: exec roundtables/dinners (8-12 peers, no pitch), owned micro-events
over big-booth sponsorships, hands-on workshops. Measure pipeline influenced per event
and stage-velocity change of attending accounts - never badge scans.

MARKETING-SOURCED vs INFLUENCED - and its politics:
- SOURCED: marketing created the opp's first touch. Clean but undercounts - enterprise
  deals have 10-25 touches; "first touch" is an accounting fiction.
- INFLUENCED: marketing touched the account during the cycle. Overcounts - everything
  gets "influenced" and the number becomes propaganda.
THE POLITICS: sales claims sourced deals "were coming anyway"; marketing claims
influence on everything; comp plans amplify both. SETTLEMENT: report BOTH numbers
under fixed definitions signed by CRO + CMO, and judge marketing on pipeline-per-₹
plus the win-rate/velocity delta of marketed vs unmarketed accounts - a holdout of
untouched lookalike accounts is the only honest referee.

MDF (MARKET DEVELOPMENT FUNDS) WITH PARTNERS:
- 1-3% of partner-sourced revenue back as co-marketing budget, claim-based (proposal
  → approval → proof of execution → reimbursement) - never a blank cheque
- Fund activities that create NET-NEW pipeline (joint webinars, co-branded campaigns,
  partner-hosted events), not the partner's own brand ads
- Track pipeline per MDF ₹ by partner; typical MDF utilization runs <60% - reallocate
  unclaimed or unproductive funds quarterly to partners who convert
```

## ⛔ Marketing Failure Modes

```
⛔ CHANNEL SPRAY - five channels at 20% competence; none reaches escape velocity.
⛔ BLENDED-CAC BLINDNESS - scaling spend on an average while the margin is underwater.
⛔ VANITY ATTRIBUTION - "influenced pipeline" as the headline; nobody believes the number.
⛔ BRAND SPEND ON A LEAKY FUNNEL - buying awareness before activation works (<40%).
⛔ PREMATURE DIVERSIFICATION - adding channel 2 to escape channel 1's hard lessons.
⛔ ABM WITHOUT TIERS - 1:1 budget spread across 500 accounts; bespoke for nobody.
⛔ KILLING SEO AT MONTH 4 - judging a 12-month channel on a 3-month window.
```

## Example

**User says:** "₹6L/month budget. We're on Meta, Google, LinkedIn, a podcast, and just
signed an events sponsorship. CAC is creeping up. What do we cut?"

**Actions:**
1. **Constraints:** ₹6L/mo across 5 channels ≈ ₹1.2L each - below minimum effective
   scale for most (§6); B2B SaaS, ACV ₹4L; max allowable CAC ₹1L at target payback;
   only Google search currently pays back inside 6 months.
2. **Options:** (a) keep all five, trim each - preserves optionality, guarantees five
   sub-scale channels; (b) all-in on Google search - cleanest CAC, but impression
   share is already 74%, so the ceiling is near; (c) 70/20/10 per §7 - core Google,
   scale LinkedIn (best early CVR signal; ICP reachable there), one experiment; kill
   the podcast and exit the events deal at its break clause.
3. **Trade-offs → recommendation:** (c). ₹4.2L Google with marginal-CAC monitoring
   (§7 rule: two months of marginal CAC > ₹1L → stop scaling); ₹1.2L LinkedIn behind
   a 90-day pass/kill gate with CPL and demo-rate thresholds written NOW; ₹60K to one
   experiment. Podcast and events showed no attributable pipeline in two quarters -
   sunk cost, not core.
4. **Risks / reversal condition:** Google saturates before LinkedIn matures →
   mitigate by seeding SEO/content from proven search terms now (same intent,
   compounding cost curve). Events relationship value lost → revisit as §9 field
   marketing (own a 10-person roundtable, skip the booth). REVERSAL: if LinkedIn
   misses its 90-day gates, its budget moves to content/SEO - not back to a
   five-way spray.

**Result:** A 70/20/10 reallocation with named kill criteria, marginal-CAC guardrails,
and a successor channel (SEO) seeded before the core channel saturates.

**Quality check:** Does every channel have a max CAC, payback target, and a written
kill/scale rule? Is marginal (not blended) CAC the scaling signal? Is exactly one core
channel being saturated rather than five being sampled?
