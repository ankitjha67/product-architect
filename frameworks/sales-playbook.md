# Sales Playbook Framework

Repeatable selling is a system, not a personality. This playbook gives you the
ICP canvas, the stage model with exit criteria, the qualification frameworks
(MEDDICC/BANT/SPICED), discovery and demo scripts, objection handling, the
mutual action plan, pipeline math, forecasting, comp, and the dashboard - all
copy-paste ready.

## 2. SALES STAGE MODEL - WITH EXIT CRITERIA

Stages are defined by what the BUYER has done, not what the rep hopes. A deal
only advances when the exit criteria are objectively met.

```
| # | Stage          | Exit criteria (must be TRUE to advance)                  | Default % |
|---|----------------|----------------------------------------------------------|-----------|
| 0 | Lead/Prospect  | Fits ICP; contact info verified                          | -         |
| 1 | Qualified      | Pain + fit confirmed in conversation; meeting booked     | 10%       |
| 2 | Discovery      | Pain, impact, timeline, decision process documented      | 20%       |
| 3 | Demo/Eval      | Champion identified; tailored demo done; next step set   | 40%       |
| 4 | Proposal       | Pricing presented; economic buyer engaged; MAP started   | 60%       |
| 5 | Negotiation    | Verbal yes; redlines/terms in progress; close date firm  | 80%       |
| 6 | Closed-Won     | Signed contract; payment terms agreed                    | 100%      |
| - | Closed-Lost    | Documented reason + competitor (feeds win/loss analysis) | 0%        |

RULE: If you can't prove the exit criteria, the deal is NOT in that stage.
"Sandbagging" (deals stuck) and "happy ears" (inflated stages) both kill forecasts.
```

## 3. QUALIFICATION FRAMEWORKS

### MEDDICC deep-dive (best for complex B2B / enterprise)

```
| Letter | Meaning            | The questions to answer                          |
|--------|--------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| M | Metrics             | What measurable outcome? (₹ saved, % gained, time)|
| E | Economic Buyer      | Who controls budget and can sign? Have we met them?|
| D | Decision Criteria   | What technical/business criteria will they judge on?|
| D | Decision Process    | What are the exact steps & timeline to a signature?|
| I | Identify Pain       | What's the compelling pain? What if they do nothing?|
| C | Champion            | Who sells for us internally & has influence?       |
| C | Competition         | Who/what else (incl. status quo / build-in-house)? |

MEDDICC SCORECARD (score each 0-2: 0=unknown, 1=partial, 2=confirmed):
  Metrics ___ EconBuyer ___ DecCriteria ___ DecProcess ___
  Pain ___ Champion ___ Competition ___        TOTAL: ___/14
  <7 → unqualified, don't forecast.  7-11 → working deal.  12-14 → commit.
```

### BANT (fast, transactional / SMB)
```
Budget - can they afford it?   Authority - are we talking to the decider?
Need - is the pain real?       Timeline - when must this be solved?
Use when: short cycles, low ACV, single decision-maker.
```

### SPICED (modern, outcome-led - Winning by Design)
```
Situation - current state    Pain - the problem + impact
Impact - quantified business consequence of the pain
Critical event - a date forcing action (compliance, contract end, launch)
Decision - process & criteria
Use when: consultative, value-selling motions where outcomes drive the deal.
```

**When to use which:** BANT for velocity/SMB, MEDDICC for enterprise/complex
committees, SPICED when you sell business outcomes and need a "why now."

## 4. DISCOVERY CALL SCRIPT & QUESTION BANK

```
STRUCTURE (30-45 min):
  0-3 min   Rapport + agenda: "I'd love to understand your world, share how we
            help teams like yours, and figure out together if it's a fit. Fair?"
  3-25 min  Discovery (talk <30% of the time - they should talk 70%)
  25-35 min Tailored value framing based on what you heard
  35-40 min Mutual next step (book it live, don't "follow up")

QUESTION BANK:
  Situation:  "Walk me through how you handle [process] today."
              "What tools/workarounds are involved?"
  Pain:       "What's the hardest part of that?"
              "What made you take this call now?"  ← surfaces the trigger
  Impact:     "What does that cost you - in time, money, or risk?"
              "If nothing changes in 6 months, what happens?"
  Decision:   "Who else would weigh in on a decision like this?"
              "What does your evaluation/buying process usually look like?"
  Criteria:   "What would this need to do to be a clear win for you?"

NEVER: pitch before you've found the pain. A demo without discovery is a
feature dump that converts at a fraction of the rate.
```

## 5. DEMO BEST PRACTICES

```
TELL-SHOW-TELL (per feature):
  TELL  - "You mentioned [their pain]. Here's how we solve it."
  SHOW  - demonstrate the exact path to that outcome (their data if possible)
  TELL  - "So that means [their metric] improves because [reason]."

DO-NO-HARM RULES:
  ✓ Demo only what maps to discovered pain (3-5 things, not the whole product)
  ✓ Start with the "wow" outcome, then show how - not a UI tour
  ✓ Use their language, their use case, ideally their logo/data in a sandbox
  ✓ Pause for reactions: "How does that compare to how you do it today?"
  ⛔ Don't show features they didn't ask about ("happy to dig into X later")
  ⛔ Don't wing a feature you're unsure of (a bug live kills trust)
  ⛔ Don't end without a confirmed next step
```

## 6. OBJECTION-HANDLING MATRIX

```
Acknowledge → Reframe → Prove → Advance.  Never argue.

| Objection                  | Reframe                              | Proof to deploy            |
|----------------------------|--------------------------------------|----------------------------|
| "Too expensive"            | Price vs cost of the pain            | ROI calc, payback months   |
| "We'll build it ourselves" | Opportunity cost + maintenance       | TCO comparison, time-to-value|
| "No budget right now"      | What's the cost of waiting?          | Critical event, quick win  |
| "Happy with current tool"  | What would have to be true to switch?| Differentiator + migration ease|
| "Need to think about it"   | What specifically is unresolved?     | Address the real concern   |
| "Send me info"             | Brush-off detector → re-discover     | Offer a tailored next step |
| "Not the right time"       | Surface the real blocker             | Champion + critical event  |
| "Have to check with [X]"   | Loop them in now                     | Multi-thread the deal      |

TECHNIQUE: "Feel, Felt, Found" - "I understand why you feel that way; other
[role]s felt the same; what they found was [outcome]."
```

## 7. PROPOSAL & PRICING PRESENTATION

```
PROPOSAL STRUCTURE (1-3 pages, never a 40-slide deck):
  1. Their goals & pain (mirror their words - proves you listened)
  2. Recommended solution (mapped to each pain)
  3. Expected outcomes / ROI (quantified)
  4. Investment (pricing - anchor to value, present highest tier first)
  5. Implementation plan / timeline
  6. Next steps (the MAP)

PRESENT PRICING LIVE, never just emailed. Frame value before number:
  "Based on the ₹[X] this is costing you, here's the investment..."
  Anchor high → present the recommended package, then options below it.
  Default to annual; show monthly only if asked (creates a deferral).
```

## 8. MUTUAL ACTION PLAN (Close Plan)

```
Co-built WITH the champion. Working backward from their go-live date.

| # | Step                          | Owner (us/them) | Due date | Status |
|---|-------------------------------|-----------------|----------|--------|
| 1 | Technical validation / POC    | them + SE       | __/__    | ☐      |
| 2 | Security review               | them            | __/__    | ☐      |
| 3 | Pricing & proposal review     | us              | __/__    | ☐      |
| 4 | Legal / MSA redlines          | both            | __/__    | ☐      |
| 5 | Economic buyer sign-off       | them            | __/__    | ☐      |
| 6 | Signature                     | them            | __/__    | ☐      |
| 7 | Kickoff / onboarding handoff  | us              | __/__    | ☐      |

WHY IT WORKS: surfaces hidden steps (security, legal), confirms the real
timeline, and tests champion commitment ("Can we walk through the steps to
go live by [date]?"). A buyer who won't build a MAP with you isn't ready.
```

## 9. NEGOTIATION GUARDRAILS & DISCOUNT APPROVAL MATRIX

```
GUARDRAILS:
  ✓ Never discount without a concession in return (longer term, case study,
    upfront payment, multi-year, reference, faster signature)
  ✓ Hold price until late - discount is your last lever, not your first
  ✓ Trade, don't cave: "I can do [discount] IF we sign by [date] and do 2-year"
  ⛔ Never negotiate against yourself (don't drop price twice unprompted)
  ⛔ Never let price drop without the deal advancing in return

DISCOUNT APPROVAL MATRIX:
| Discount        | Approver        | Required justification              |
|-----------------|-----------------|-------------------------------------|
| 0-10%           | Rep             | Standard, logged in CRM             |
| 10-20%          | Sales Manager   | Concession received + close date    |
| 20-30%          | VP Sales        | Strategic logo / multi-year / volume|
| >30%            | CRO / CEO       | Exception case, written rationale   |
```

```

  ☐ Implementation timeline & any commitments made

RULE: Sales owns the outcome they promised. Over-selling here = churn later.
See customer-journey.md (onboarding deep-dive) and Agent 17 for retention plays.
```

## 11. PIPELINE MATH

```
KEY FORMULAS:
  Pipeline coverage   = open pipeline ($) ÷ quota for period   (target 3-4x)
  Win rate            = deals won ÷ deals worked (or created)
  Avg sales cycle     = mean days from stage 1 → closed-won
  Avg deal size (ACV) = total ACV won ÷ deals won

  SALES VELOCITY = (# opportunities × win rate × avg deal size) ÷ sales cycle days
       = revenue generated per day. Improve ANY of the 4 levers to grow.

WORKED EXAMPLE:
  40 opps × 25% win × ₹6,00,000 ACV ÷ 60 days = ₹1,00,000/day velocity.
  Raise win rate 25%→30% → velocity ₹1,20,000/day (+20%) with zero new leads.

COVERAGE EXAMPLE:
  Quarter quota ₹3 Cr, win rate 25% → need ₹12 Cr+ pipeline (4x) to hit it.
```

## 12. FORECASTING CATEGORIES

```
| Category    | Meaning                                   | Confidence |
|-------------|-------------------------------------------|------------|
| Commit      | Rep stakes reputation it closes this period| ~90%       |
| Best Case   | Could close with everything going right    | ~50%       |
| Pipeline    | In-quarter but needs work                  | ~20%       |
| Omitted     | Not this period                            | -          |

FORECAST CALL DISCIPLINE: each Commit deal must have MEDDICC ≥12, a MAP,
economic-buyer engagement, and a firm close date. Forecast = Commit + a
risk-adjusted slice of Best Case. Track forecast accuracy weekly.
```

## 13. SALES COMP BASICS

```
OTE (On-Target Earnings) = Base + Variable (commission at 100% of quota)
  Common split: 50/50 (field/enterprise) to 60/40 or 70/30 (SMB/inside).
  Quota ≈ 4-6x OTE (so a rep covers their fully-loaded cost several times over).

COMMISSION STRUCTURE:
  Base rate on bookings (e.g. 10% of ACV) + ACCELERATORS above quota
  (e.g. 1.5x on attainment 100-120%, 2x above 120%) to reward overperformance.
  Add SPIFs for strategic priorities (new logos, target product, multi-year).
  Clawback for churn within X months (aligns sales with retention).

⚠️ Comp plans are powerful behavior levers - design with finance and review
annually. Plan terms can have legal/tax implications; see the disclaimer.
```

```
| Ramp time (new rep)     | <ICP cycle × 1.5         |
| Lead→opp conversion     | tracked by source        |
| Forecast accuracy       | within ±10%              |
| Net Revenue Retention   | >100% (>120% elite)      |
```
